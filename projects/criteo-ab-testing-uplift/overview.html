A/B Testing with Uplift Modeling and Causal Decomposition

Criteo Uplift Dataset · 13.9M Users

Live App Code

A real-world RCT of an online ad campaign randomly assigned 13.9M users to treatment (eligible for ad auctions) or control (never shown ads). Only 3.6% of treated users actually saw an ad. The other 96.4% were assigned but never exposed.

Standard uplift models work: four meta-learners estimate Conditional Average Treatment Effect (CATE), and the X-Learner ranks users well (Qini: 0.1753, top decile converts at 5x control). But with 96.4% non-compliance, what is the CATE score actually picking up? A high score could mean the user is easy to reach, or that the user genuinely responds to ads. CATE blends both into one number. The ranking is fixed regardless of whether the business goal is maximizing conversions, minimizing cost per acquisition, or hitting a target ROI.

Using instrumental variables, CATE decomposes into reachability P(E) and responsiveness LATE(X). The result is two scores per user instead of one. Reachable users need lower bids. Responsive users are worth a premium. CATE treats them identically. The decomposition trades 10% ranking performance for the ability to act on why each user scores high.
